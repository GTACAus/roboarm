03/09

Connecting the sensor cables

Connect the sensor's three cables to the board's pins IO32, GND and 3V3. Your cables may be one of three colour sets: match yours with the picture. GND and 3V3 are the pair of pins right next to the USB-C socket. IO32 is in the same row as GND, four pins further along.

04/09

Tap 1 GND, 2 3V3 and 3 IO32 to watch each plug go on. Drag the model to turn it.

05/09

Connecting the sensor cables

  1. Find the pin header and the USB-C socket at one end of it.
  2. Push the ground cable onto GND: the inner pin of the pair next to the USB-C socket.
  3. Push the power cable onto 3V3: the outer pin of that same pair.
  4. Push the signal cable onto IO32: the inner row, four pins further along from GND.
  5. Check each cable is pushed all the way down and on the right pin.

07/09

Attach your myosensor

Attach the sensor to your forearm before you start the arm program. The board measures your relaxed muscle when it starts.

08/09

Start the arm program on the hub

Relax your arm and keep it still while the program starts. Tap the numbers under the hub to go through each step.

09/09

Move the arm with your muscle

  1. Squeeze your muscle: the motor shown on the hub turns. Relax: it goes back home.
  2. Press the hub's left or right button to choose a different motor.
  3. With an iPad: join your board's Wi-Fi (for example GTAC 12, password robotarm) and open the Muscle sensor tab. You will see your muscle signal as a graph, choose the motor, and use Amplify if the arm barely moves.
